Healthcare · Research · Data

A different perspective on data and healthcare.

I'm drawn to the spaces where healthcare, people, technology, and data intersect. Each offers a different way of understanding health, but I find the most interesting questions often emerge when we consider them together.

I'm particularly interested in how we can use those connections to better understand what shapes our health, identify opportunities for prevention, and help people make more informed decisions about their well-being.

A little about me

Different disciplines. Shared questions.

My academic and professional experiences have taken me across population health, biomedical data science, and business. At first glance, these fields might seem distinct. I've come to appreciate how much they have to teach one another.

Through public health research, I've explored the broader conditions that shape health across communities. Human factors research introduced me to the ways people interact with technology, environments, and complex systems. In healthcare analytics, I've had the opportunity to work closer to the decisions themselves, using data to understand performance, identify opportunities, and help organizations think differently about the challenges they face.

What connects these experiences is a curiosity about how things work beneath the surface. I'm interested in looking beyond the immediate problem, understanding the factors that contribute to it, and finding opportunities to do something differently. Whether that's improving how we understand population health or helping individuals take a more proactive approach to their well-being, I believe some of the most valuable insights come from connecting perspectives that are too often considered separately.

How I think

My approach.

I'm interested not only in understanding how things work, but in what becomes possible when we look at them differently.

01

Look beneath the surface

The most visible part of a problem isn't always the most important. I like to explore the underlying patterns, relationships, and circumstances that shape what we see.

02

Connect different perspectives

Complex problems rarely belong to one discipline. I look for ways to bring together research, data, technology, and the experiences of the people closest to a problem.

03

Turn understanding into possibility

Understanding a problem is only part of the process. I'm interested in what that understanding makes possible, whether that's a new way of thinking, a better system, or an opportunity to prevent a problem before it begins.
