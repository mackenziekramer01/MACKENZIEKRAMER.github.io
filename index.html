Healthcare · Data Analytics · Research

Data beyond the science.

I'm drawn to the spaces where healthcare, people, technology, and data intersect. I find that each area offers a different way of understanding health, but the most interesting questions often emerge when we consider them together.

I'm particularly interested in how we can use those connections to better understand what shapes our health, identify opportunities for prevention, and help people make more informed decisions about their well-being.

A little about me

Different disciplines, connected questions.

My academic and professional experiences have taken me across population health, biomedical data science, and business administration. At first these fields felt distinct, but as I continue to grow I've come to appreciate how much they have to teach one another.

Through public health research, I've explored the broader social and environmental conditions that shape health across communities. Human factors research introduced me to the ways people interact with technology and its implications for adoption and performance. In healthcare analytics, I've had the opportunity to work closer to the decisions themselves, using data to understand utilization, identify cost-savings opportunities, and help organizations think differently about the challenges they face.

What connects these experiences is a curiosity about how things work beneath the surface. I'm interested in looking beyond the immediate problem, understanding the factors that contribute to it, and finding opportunities to do something differently. Whether that's improving how we understand population health or helping individuals take a more proactive approach to their well-being, I believe some of the most valuable insights come from connecting perspectives that are too often considered separately.

How I think

My approach.

I'm interested not only in understanding how things work, but in what becomes possible when we look at them differently.

01

Look beneath the surface

The most visible part of a problem isn't always the most important. I like to explore the underlying patterns, relationships, and circumstances that shape what we see.

02

Connect different perspectives

Complex problems rarely belong to one discipline. I look for ways to bring together research, data, technology, and the experiences of the people closest to a problem.

03

Put insight into practice

Meaningful change takes more than identifying what could be improved. I'm interested in how ideas translate into action, from bringing the right people into the process to navigating implementation and helping new approaches become part of the way an organization works.
